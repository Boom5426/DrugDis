DrugDis / component-resolved evaluation

Disentangling general and context‑specific effects in drug‑response prediction.

Aggregate prediction scores do not distinguish general response tendencies from context-specific effects.

DrugDis applies an exact orthogonal decomposition to measured and predicted responses on the same observed drug–sample pairs, separating additive effects from drug–sample interactions, and evaluates interaction direction, amplitude and prediction error separately.

Core principle

Separate the additive. Score the interaction.

01 / Framework

Four quantities should not be collapsed into one score.

DrugDis decomposes measured and predicted responses with one operator built on the observed pairs, then reports each component separately: how much of the response it carries, whether a prediction recovers its direction, how large the predicted component is and where the squared error lies, and how much of it repeated measurements reproduce.

01 / Orthogonal decomposition

Which part of the response is additive, and which is interaction?

On the observed drug–sample pairs, the total response splits exactly into an additive component, the best additive fit of drug and sample marginals, and a drug–sample interaction component orthogonal to it.

The interaction component is defined relative to the observed pairs.

03 / Additive dominance

Additive effects dominate response variance in every large resource, not only in NCI60.

04 / Evidence

Aggregate accuracy can hide how little of the interaction a model recovers.

The baseline M0 is a dual-tower predictor of ECFP4 fingerprints and gene-level expression. Scored component by component, the same predictions tell different stories under the three distribution shifts.

Held-out cell lines

A total-response correlation of 0.86 recovers interactions at 0.32.

On the same predictions, additive correlation was 0.96 and interaction correlation 0.32. Interaction error accounted for 77% of M0's squared error, and on matched supports additive recovery was 1.00 times its cross-assay reference while interaction recovery was 0.37 times its own.

Held-out compounds

Holding out compounds moves the error into the additive component.

The additive component absorbed 64% of squared error while occupying about 2% of the evaluation degrees of freedom; its error density was 87–92 times that of the interaction component. Additive correlation fell from 0.96 to 0.70, interaction correlation from 0.32 to 0.29.

Zero-shot organoids

A new biological system and new inputs lower both components.

M0 correlations fell to 0.61 for additive effects and 0.07 for interactions. Pooling in two input-incompatible cohorts inflated the apparent M4–M0 change in additive correlation from +0.007 to +0.145, so the primary effect is reported on the prespecified three-cohort support.

Targeted intervention

Component supervision improves interaction direction in selected regimes.

M3 and M4 share one two-head architecture with 20,029,444 trainable parameters; only M4 receives separate additive and interaction training targets, so the M3–M4 contrast isolates component supervision.

M4 raised interaction correlation in all five seeds under held-out compounds and in zero-shot organoids, but interaction R² rose consistently only under held-out compounds. In organoids, interaction R² stayed below zero for every arm: improved direction did not recover interaction magnitude.

A different selection rule is not by itself a better one.

Six criteria selected M0 or M4 from the same GDSC1 predictions, and each choice was tested on GDSC2 without refitting. Interaction correlation chose differently from aggregate correlation in two of five held-out-cell-line seeds and four of five held-out-compound seeds, but those choices did not reduce the prespecified GDSC2 selectivity error; mean absolute error rose by 0.00245 and 0.00043.

05 / Interpretation

The same aggregate score can rest on very different response structure.

DrugDis does not replace prediction metrics with a composite score. It states which component a score reflects, where the remaining error lies, and how that compares with what repeated measurements reproduce.

I

Aggregate is not interaction.

Because additive effects carry most of the variance, a model can reach a high total-response correlation while recovering little of the drug–sample interaction.

II

The interaction lives on the observed pairs.

It is the deviation from the best additive fit to the measured pairs. Changing the compounds, samples or pairs changes it; it is not an intrinsic biological interaction.

III

A reference is not a ceiling.

Cross-assay reproducibility indicates how much of each component repeated measurements share on matched supports. It frames recovery; it does not bound what a model can attain.

06 / Software

Score your own predictions component by component.

The evaluation API needs NumPy, pandas and SciPy. It takes one row per observed drug–sample pair, builds the operator on that support, and returns each component's correlation, amplitude and error together with the exact error attribution.

From a response table to a component profile.

Run the bundled synthetic demo for an interface check, or pass your own test pairs with measured and predicted responses. The repository also holds every script, split manifest and result table behind the manuscript.

evaluate.py
from drugdis.evaluate import component_profile

scores = component_profile(
    test_frame,          # SMILES, Sample_ID per row
    y="Sensitivity",
    yhat="prediction",
)

print(scores["rawPCC"], scores["sharedPCC"],
      scores["intPCC"], scores["R2_interaction"])
